# Lessons

per lesson Thermo has learned from its own recorded studies, probes and reviews. A lesson here restates what a report or
research note already says; it is not new evidence. The evidence class in each
entry is copied from the cited source. Newest first.*

## How to add an entry

One entry per lesson, in the table below. The `Evidence class` column uses the
cited source's own words (`exact_reference`, `software_simulation`,
exploration, review). A lesson from a negative result is stated as negative.
Link the report or note that states it; the knowledge-base checker verifies
links in every file under `docs/knowledge/`. Do not add entries for results
that have no report or note yet.

## What Thermo has learned so far

A short map of the entries below, grouped by line of work. Every point is
restated from a cited report or note; the entries carry the numbers and links.

- **The samplers do what the math says.** THRML's spin sampler (E0), a
  compiled M5 kernel (E0 stage B) and its categorical sampler (Potts stage A)
  all match Thermo's exact one-sweep matrices at small sweep counts, including
  clamping, block order and three-colour schedules. Torx 0.0.2 passes the
  pinned 0.0.1 contract test.
- **The conservation line (M1 to M4I) is closed, negative.** Small loss gains
  (M1, M2, M4) did not become faithful whole-program execution. Local fidelity
  improvements traded against path survival (M4C to M4G and the fixtures),
  and the best result, 89.61% survival at cap 4 (M4I), fell short of 95%
  because a fixed parameter cap bounded it structurally.
- **The meta-EBM line (M5a to M5c) works on reading B.** Reading B is the
  standing prefactor choice. Compiled kernels fit the published local rule once
  degree is repaired, at about 1.5x the parity-bound input copies. A finite
  inner K can keep a good stationary law and still slow inference. Execution
  costs remain open.
- **No sampler wins everywhere.** Tempering helps on biased or trapping
  targets, independent Gibbs wins on small denoising posteriors, symmetry
  estimators are free gains at zero field, and conditional averaging helps in
  weak regimes. Better probabilities do not always mean better decisions.
- **For Potts targets, default to tempering plus label symmetry** at budgets
  of 1024 sweeps and above. It is decisively better when chains trap and costs
  at most one fourfold step when they mix. A pilot-based switching policy did
  not beat that default.
- **Adaptive policies have now failed twice** against simple baselines
  (changing-evidence restarts, Potts stage C). A third needs the owner's
  decision.
- **Associative memory:** a categorical-hidden dense memory far outstrips
  Hebbian Hopfield, and on pairwise binary hardware, binary hidden units with a
  negative bias stand in for the categorical unit at equilibrium at modest
  coupling range; one-hot inhibition is range-hungry and domain-wall chains
  don't mix. Whether a binary design matches a native categorical unit within a
  sweep budget is open: the sampled categorical reference barely mixes at the
  cold β its equilibrium needs, whatever its update order (A2).
- **Process:** list fixed choices and check whether any caps the metric,
  including budget-grid floors and noise floors. Size negative controls on the
  exact side, and run a minutes-long probe before any preflight layer. Commit
  everything before a run. Replay numerically rather than re-hashing
  recomputed floats, and decide on algorithmic budgets rather than timings.
