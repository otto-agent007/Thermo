# Exact-seed residual estimator: useful selectively, not a reliability certificate

## Implemented method
For a fixed deduplicated feasible seed set S chosen before retained AIS paths, compute its exact target mass Z_S. Estimate the remainder using AIS weights W times indicator(final endpoint not in S):

Z_residual = Z_S + mean[W * 1{X_final outside S}].

This is NOT clipping an ordinary estimate to a bound and NOT using whether a state was ever visited. The finite-state weighted AIS endpoint identity implies unbiasedness under valid normalized starting density and invariant kernels. Replacing a sampled contribution with its known mean does not guarantee variance reduction because component covariance matters.

Independent tests verify the complete weighted final-state vector, residual expectation, actual production endpoint/weight replay, and a counterexample: with a perfect proposal ordinary importance weights are constant (zero variance), while replacing seed contributions this way introduces variance. Six tests passed overall.

The estimator satisfies the known-seed lower bound automatically, but that is now built into its formula and no longer evidence that the unseeded mass was estimated correctly. Some runs have zero nonseed endpoints; on the favorable near-complete-seed fixture this can be harmless, while for other problems it could conceal missing mass. No confidence calibration was established for residual estimates; the previous failed t/ESS rule cannot be assumed repaired by changing estimator.

## Conclusion
The residual estimator is mathematically sound and useful when known seeds account for substantial probability, but neither uniformly lower variance nor unknown-mode protection follows. Keep it as an experimental option, retain ordinary estimates for comparison, and investigate residual-specific uncertainty/coverage before considering a d5 efficacy expansion.
