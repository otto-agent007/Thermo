# Independent affine/AIS mathematical review

Reviewed `affine.py` reconstruction on 2026-11-13. Scope is generic algebra and synthetic distributions, not saved d5 noisy observations, scientific efficacy, DEM extraction fidelity, or pilot authorization.

## Verdict

Core algebra is mathematically consistent for finite, strictly interior probabilities and a numerically full-support proposal. This is **not** evidence the finite-budget sampler estimates d5 class masses accurately. The 8 independent tests in `test_independent.py` passed. The author's 4 tests additionally exhaust all binary 2-by-3 matrices/fibers and include a Monte Carlo synthetic check.

- RREF with high-probability pivot preference yields one offset plus an injective null-space-coordinate map. Summing independent Bernoulli fault probabilities over that fiber gives the joint syndrome/logical mass, not a probability normalized within that fiber.
- Product Bernoulli density on free coordinates has known normalizer 1. Do not add a `2**nullity` factor or assume uniform free coordinates when initializing from Bernoulli.
- AIS weights are updated before the transition at each new beta, using `(log_target - log_base) / steps`. This is correct for a linear geometric bridge.
- Coordinate toggles are symmetric proposals. Their Metropolis acceptance is correct for the bridge density; numerical density increments match direct product probabilities.
- Final log mass is log-mean-exp of weights. The mean mass estimator is unbiased under exact initialization/invariant kernels; its logarithm generally is not unbiased.

## Independent checks

`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q test_independent.py`

## Required robustness follow-up before freezing

Reported to implementer: NaN probabilities evade the existing range predicate, and extreme probability/gamma combinations can round proposal q to 0 or 1 despite input probabilities being interior. Require finite probability/gamma validation and a stable full-support proposal guard, plus regression tests. Also reject nonbinary/malformed matrix inputs rather than silently converting unexpected floats if the public API is meant for arbitrary callers.

## Scientific limitations and pilot implications

- High importance-weight ESS does not prove mode coverage. All particles can miss a decisive region and still show high ESS. Low ESS triggers fallback, but acceptable ESS alone cannot certify an AIS-derived correction.
- The same randomly selected coordinate is applied to all particles at each move. Individual chains have valid kernels, but shared random schedules can correlate particles. The usual weight ESS remains a diagnostic, not a calibrated independent-sample count or confidence interval.
- The chosen product proposal ignores pivot-coordinate energetic effects and may be poor for nonzero syndromes. High-probability pivots and gamma sharpening are heuristics, not convergence arguments.
- There is no budget-to-convergence guarantee. Metropolis one-coordinate mixing can be slow or effectively trapped on relevant time scales.
- Proposal choices, seeds, fallback thresholds, schedules and actual sample IDs must be frozen before inspecting pilot outcomes. Report fallback frequency and both repairs and harms against correlated matching.
- A 192-shot technical pilot is chiefly an engineering/feasibility check, especially at small failure rates. It cannot establish a practical advantage by itself.
- No d5 efficacy experiment was run during this review.
