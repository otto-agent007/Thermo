# Independent reference-feasibility review

## Model fidelity

`model_fidelity.py/json` verifies all six archived cells against their source
circuits. Conversion with decomposition disabled, conversion with decomposition
enabled, and saved DEMs have identical complete Bernoulli fault factors. Disjoint
approximation is explicitly disabled. Every model has 397 distinct fault columns,
120 detectors and one logical bit. The decomposed representation has 100 separator
markers and complete faults can affect four detectors. These correlations remain
intact when each whole error instruction is one variable.

## External small exact reference

Cheng and Pan, Appendix D of
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39556v1 supplies a nine-qubit independent-X fixture.
`external_fixture.py/json` uses both a 512-state physical enumeration and two
16-state stabilizer-coset enumerations, with exact integer arithmetic at p=1/10.
Both give class masses 0.046268496 and 0.019431504. The four highest-probability
configurations capture 96.4826% and 98.4580% of their respective class masses.
This is an algebra/reference fixture, not evidence for noisy d5 performance.

## Positive projected-event bounds

Dropping parity constraints forms a superset of the desired event. The positive
XOR-distribution dynamic program therefore gives a full-class upper bound without
changing the noise model or splitting correlated faults. Outward elementary
arithmetic also encloses numerical rounding, assuming correctly rounded platform
operations and nextafter. The guarantee is for exact values of stored binary64
probabilities, not uncertainty in physical noise parameters.

Five independent tests in `qec-reference/test_bounds_independent.py` use exact
Fraction arithmetic over stored binary64 values. They verify interval containment
for all targets of small dependent-parity models, projected supersets, p=0/1,
extreme probabilities, duplicate-candidate removal and the external fixture.
The last distinguishes stored binary64 p=.1 from exact decimal p=1/10.

`audit_saved_bounds.py/json` independently checks all 32 candidate assignments
against complete DEM instruction supports, exactly computes their probabilities
as rational numbers, verifies interval enclosure and checks captured-fraction
interval arithmetic. All pass. The 32 solver runs report optimal status, though
candidate mass validity does not rely on their order or optimality.

## What the saved d5 result establishes

For the known harmful case, the 16 class-0 candidates carry at least
1.04514939722e-14, while its 18-check relaxed upper bound is 4.33009998944e-13.
Class-1 has lower bound 1.29239011916e-20 and upper 1.19899571795e-13.
The class intervals overlap: no winning-class certificate follows.

The 16-candidate captured fraction is bounded only by [2.41368%,100%] in class0
and approximately [0.00001078%,100%] in class1. The first four candidates account
for nearly all of the *known 16-candidate* mass in class0, but this must not be
mistaken for nearly all full-class mass. The bounds are useful and honest, but
currently too loose to measure d5 coverage precisely.

## Tensor/resource findings

The existing minimum-fill order is a heuristic, not a lower bound on optimal
contraction cost. Its dual width29 calls for an 8GiB float64 or16GiB longdouble
largest product, before other live data. At inspection, visible memory was9.7GiB
with9.1GiB available and no swap; cgroup memory limits were not exposed.
Unsliced allocation is unsafe. The eight-variable sliced plan's largest
longdouble product is256MiB; sequential slices and online accumulation offer a
much safer memory path, with increased work. Actual timed slice/RSS measurement
belongs to the implementation worker and was not repeated here.

This platform's longdouble has64-bit significand, not IEEE binary128. The signed
Fourier formulation can lose relative accuracy on tiny class masses. A completed
untruncated contraction is not automatically a trustworthy mass reference:
rounding/cancellation must be assessed separately. Higher memory does not remove
that numerical issue. No claim that exact d5 computation is impossible is justified
by this one heuristic plan.
